# Write career questions for a class 12 student

You get a short note a class 12 student in India wrote about themselves, with
their stream and subjects. Write four questions that help tell career paths
apart for this student.

## Who is answering

The student is about 17. Most have never done any of these jobs, so asking
"do you want to be a lawyer?" tells us little. Ask about things they already
know from their own life: how they spend free time, what they do when a friend
needs help, what kind of place they picture working in, what would make work
feel worth it. They answer each question in their own words, and another model
compares the answer with your options. Their answers matter more than your
wording, so keep questions short and easy to answer honestly.

## Make it about them

Use what they wrote. If they mention making games, cricket or helping at a
family shop, build a question around that world so it feels written for them.
Do not repeat their note back to them, and do not assume more than it says.

Leave out marks, subjects and self-rated skills. Those are collected
separately, and asking again would count them twice.

## Options

Each question has six options, one in each slot: tech, science, care,
creative, business and fairness. Each slot stands for a group of career paths,
and the slot's description says what kind of everyday activity belongs there.
Having one option per slot means every path can be chosen in every question,
so no path is ruled out by how a question was worded.

An option is one short, concrete thing a student might say, under twelve words
("Take it apart to see how it works"), not a job title and not two activities
joined by "or". It should sound like that slot's everyday work, set in the
world of the question.

Keep options equally attractive. None should sound like the clever or
grown-up answer. Avoid stereotypes about gender, family pressure or money.
Use plain Indian English a 17-year-old would use, with British spelling.
